# Resonance

**Resonance is a game design R&D laboratory devoted to one central tension: how can complex fictional experiences become accessible without being reduced in order to become playable?**

Its proposal is not simply that a game should be “simple”, nor that rules should merely reflect theme. It starts from a harder distinction: **fictional complexity and the complication of practice are not the same thing.**

A small number of principles, relations, or transformations can generate great complexity. Conversely, a game may require a great deal of memorization, preparation, improvisation, or manipulation without producing any more fictional richness.

> **Do not simplify the world. Simplify the way into it.**

Resonance therefore looks for forms of **generative simplicity**: devices accessible enough to be practiced, yet capable of producing relations, consequences, ambiguities, perspectives, and transformations that do not all need to be predicted or modelled in advance.

## Where did the complexity go?

Removing a rule does not necessarily remove difficulty. It may transfer it to the GM, to player improvisation, to genre knowledge, to group negotiation, to preparation, or to a reduction in what can meaningfully happen in the fiction.

One question therefore runs through the whole laboratory:

> **When a design becomes more accessible, what happened to the complexity it seemed to remove?**

It may truly have disappeared because it was only complication. It may have been distributed, externalized into a material support, localized at the moment when it becomes relevant, or transformed into a generative structure. It may also simply have been hidden or moved onto somebody else.

## Accessibility is not only about rules

Resonance investigates the entry cost for both players and GMs: rules to learn, preparation, lookup, improvisation, fear of doing it badly, and the blank page. But another difficulty matters just as much: **fictional distance**.

Playing someone who thinks, desires, or understands the world differently from oneself can be difficult even with very few rules. The laboratory therefore also explores a still-open question:

> **How can a device help a player inhabit an alterity they could not spontaneously play, without reducing that alterity to a familiar version of themselves?**

Here a rule can become a cognitive aid: not to play instead of the player, but to help them begin acting from a perspective they have not yet internalized.

## A laboratory, not yet a Game Factory

Resonance has already produced [**Regard**](https://aleascript.github.io/regard), a TTRPG framework with a playable standard configuration; [**Glorantha Perspectives**](https://aleascript.github.io/glorantha-perspectives) and [**Scooby-Doo**](https://aleascript.github.io/scooby-doo), two games built with Regard; and [**Unmind**](https://aleascript.github.io/unmind), developed directly from Resonance without going through Regard.

These are real games and frameworks, but also **laboratory experiments**. They do not define Resonance after the fact. They test its questions.

> **Today, Resonance is the laboratory. The Game Factory is a hypothesis the laboratory is trying to test.**

The aim is no longer to discover a “Resonance recipe” that every game would reproduce. Quite the opposite: if the laboratory works, different experiments should be able to discover radically different procedures while attacking the same tension between accessibility, complication, and fictional generative power.
