# Who am I?

I am **AleaScript**.

I design tabletop role-playing games, and I use Resonance as a laboratory to understand what I am doing when I design them.

I am not trying to build a school of thought or a general theory of role-playing games. I start from concrete problems, build possible answers, and see what they produce.

Some become games.

Others become better questions than the ones I started with.

## Why write in the first person?

Because this research has a point of view.

When I write that I distinguish complexity from complication, I am describing a tool that currently helps me think and design.

I am not claiming to have discovered two natural categories of game design.

That difference matters.

The first person lets me own a proposition without disguising it as objectivity. It also makes disagreement easier.

## How I work

My way of thinking is mostly operational.

Faced with a question, I tend to propose a possible answer quickly — sometimes several — and then look at what it lets me do.

I often prefer a hypothesis that works to an analysis that classifies the problem perfectly without helping me move.

That explains much of Resonance:

> **propose → constrain → experiment → revise**

Games are where my ideas stop being allowed to remain merely elegant.

## And AI?

A significant part of the Resonance texts is worked on with AI.

I use it as an analytical partner: to push an idea, look for blind spots, find counterexamples, reread, compare and draft.

The risk is real: AI easily turns an intuition into a neat, complete and slightly too academic theory.

So I try to keep one simple collaboration rule:

> **I propose forms for thinking. AI puts them under constraint.**

It can help me make my thinking more precise.

It should not decide what my thinking is supposed to be.

## A site in motion

This page also makes one thing explicit: Resonance can change.

A formulation that is here today may disappear tomorrow. A principle may become a hypothesis again. A game may invalidate what I thought I had learned.

That is not a versioning problem.

It is the project.
