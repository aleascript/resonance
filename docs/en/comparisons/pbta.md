# Apocalypse World / Powered by the Apocalypse

## Nearby question

> **How can procedures be designed from what characters actually do and what play should produce rather than from a generic engine?**

## Solution

*Apocalypse World* organizes moves, playbooks, MC principles, threats, and preparation around a specific fictional activity. Vincent Baker also describes **PbtA as an approach to game-system design**, not one engine: 2d6+stat, playbooks, or XP are common conventions, not necessary invariants.

## What it makes possible

The interface can concentrate on gestures that actually produce the intended fiction. The MC's role becomes explicit design rather than tacit expertise outside the rules.

## For Resonance

PbtA is a **major counter-test**. Resonance cannot define itself merely as “start from experience and create the system”: that approach is already powerfully explored here.

The useful question is narrower: what is gained by explicitly studying **burden distribution, access to otherness, situated relevance, and continuity across rules, materials, and table functions**? If those questions do not generate meaningful distinctions or new experiments, Resonance has no reason to claim artificial specificity.
