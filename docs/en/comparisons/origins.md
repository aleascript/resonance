# Braunstein → Blackmoor → D&D 1974

**Historical milestones:** *Braunstein* — David Wesely, **1969** · *Blackmoor* — Dave Arneson, **first sessions in 1971** · *Dungeons & Dragons* — Gary Gygax & Dave Arneson, **1974**.

## Nearby question

> **What constitutes the activity of role-playing independently of the instruments that historically accompanied it?**

Jon Peterson's historical work documents a trajectory including David Wesely's **Braunstein** games, Dave Arneson's experiments around **Blackmoor**, and the 1974 publication of *Dungeons & Dragons*.

## Historical solution

Something shifts inside wargaming: participants occupy individual positions in an adjudicated world, make decisions beyond a closed list of maneuvers, and discover consequences that may persist.

## What it makes possible

Role-playing appears as an activity broader than features later made emblematic by D&D: classes, levels, particular dice, combat structures, campaigns, or detailed sheets.

## For Resonance

This case offers a research method: **vary components of the apparatus without assuming each belongs to the essence of the medium**. Removing the GM, synchronous speech, dice, or campaign does not answer the question by definition; it lets us observe what functions those forms were actually carrying.
