# Dream Askew — Avery Alder

**Author:** Avery Alder.  
**First published version:** **2014** · revised version published with *Dream Apart* in **2018**.

## Nearby question

> **What happens to GM burden when both dice and the master are removed without sacrificing relational and community density?**

*Dream Askew* uses a **no dice, no masters** system. Players collectively build the enclave, portray characters, and also carry distributed **setting elements**. Characters have moves; **Weak Moves** earn tokens and **Strong Moves** spend them, turning vulnerability into future capacity.

## What it makes possible

The game does not replace the GM with emptiness: GM functions are **decomposed and distributed**. Setting elements give different players explicit handles on the wider world, while moves provide possible responses when someone does not know what to do. The weak/strong economy creates an alternation between vulnerability and power without random resolution.

## For Resonance

Dream Askew matters because it shows that a light interface can still contain **precise burden engineering**. “GMless” does not mean “without GM functions”; the useful question is who carries those functions, with what handles, and at what learning cost.
