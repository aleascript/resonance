# Bluebeard's Bride

**Authors:** Whitney “Strix” Beltrán, Marissa Kelly & Sarah Richardson.  
**First published:** **2017**.

## Nearby question

> **Is “one player = one character = one interiority” necessary?**

In *Bluebeard's Bride*, the participants collectively play **the Bride**, one woman composed of several psychic facets. Each player embodies a Sister — Fear, Rage, Shame, Desire, and so on — and participates from that position while exploring the house.

## What it makes possible

Internal conflict becomes a conflict of perspectives literally distributed around the table. Players need not individually simulate a contradictory psyche: the social device **externalizes and pluralizes interiority**.

That distribution introduces its own coordination cost: participants must accept shared authority over one fictional identity.

## For Resonance

Bluebeard's Bride is a strong case of **complexity made practicable through distribution**. Instead of reducing a character's internal contradiction, the game turns it into a structure of participation.
