# The Quiet Year — Avery Alder

## Nearby question

> **How can collectively creating a complex community become accessible without asking players to freely invent an entire society?**

## Solution

*The Quiet Year* combines a **collectively drawn map**, a 52-card deck corresponding to the weeks of the year, projects, resources, and events, with a known ending: the arrival of the Frost Shepherds.

## What it makes possible

The game offers few primitives, but they interact continuously. The map remembers local history; cards inject events and questions; projects provide temporality; the foretold ending creates global pressure. A politically and emotionally complex community can emerge without a prepared plot.

Much generative burden is therefore **externalized into the deck, map, and temporal structure** rather than left to moment-to-moment inspiration.

## For Resonance

This is an almost paradigmatic case of **generative simplicity**: richness is not contained in a detailed model of society but produced by interactions among a few simple forms. It also raises a demanding question: which freedoms are deliberately closed to make emergence accessible, and which remain genuinely open?
