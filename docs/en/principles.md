# Working principles {#working-principles}

These are foundations for experimentation, not a closed manifesto. They describe what Resonance currently tries to preserve across different designs.

## Start from the experience, not an inherited form {#experience-first}

A TTRPG does not have to reproduce the structures that earlier role-playing games happened to standardize. Begin with the relation to fiction and to the world that the game is trying to create, then design the procedures that make that relation possible.

## Treat the system as part of the medium {#system-as-medium}

The system is not outside the fiction looking in. Rules, conversation, uncertainty, attention and interpretation all affect what players perceive and what the fiction can become.

They cannot simply be removed. They can, however, be organized, reduced, intensified or transformed deliberately.

## Let the world and the desired experience shape the system {#world-shapes-system}

A mechanic is not valuable because it is universal. Different worlds and different experiences may call for different forms of resolution, different interfaces, or entirely different architectures.

The aim is not to make every game look like Resonance. The aim is to let each experiment discover the design it needs.

## Learn through playable experiments {#playable-experiments}

Resonance is a laboratory. Ideas become interesting when they can be embodied in play, observed, criticized and changed.

Frameworks and games produced through Resonance are therefore evidence as much as products. They may share principles without sharing mechanics.
