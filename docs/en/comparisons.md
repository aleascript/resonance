# Comparisons

Resonance uses other games as **comparison cases**, not as foils or a simple influence list.

Each subpage asks four questions:

1. **what nearby problem does the game encounter?**
2. **what concrete solution does it choose?**
3. **what does that solution make possible, and what burden does it move?**
4. **what does the case teach or leave open for Resonance?**

Similarity does not imply lineage, and lineage does not imply the same aim.

## Lineage of the medium

- [**Braunstein → Blackmoor → D&D 1974**](./comparisons/origins) — the emergence of role-playing as an activity and the distinction between a founding gesture and historical conventions.

## Language, resolution, and authority

- [**HeroQuest / QuestWorlds**](./comparisons/questworlds) — natural language as a compact semantic interface;
- [**Fate**](./comparisons/fate) — true fictional statements, permissions, and mechanical economy;
- [**Apocalypse World / PbtA**](./comparisons/pbta) — procedures designed from fictional activity; a major counter-test for Resonance's specificity;
- [**Polaris**](./comparisons/polaris) — conversation syntax and distributed authority as mechanics.

## Device, materiality, and function distribution

- [**Dread**](./comparisons/dread) — risk made physically perceptible through the tower;
- [**Ten Candles**](./comparisons/ten-candles) — light, time, and destruction as irreversible structure;
- [**Alice is Missing**](./comparisons/alice-is-missing) — silence, phones, and text as the primary medium;
- [**Bluebeard's Bride**](./comparisons/bluebeards-bride) — one fictional identity distributed across several players.

## Avery Alder: four cases especially close to the central problem

**Avery Alder's** work is especially useful for Resonance because it demonstrates several very different ways of producing dense fiction with comparatively concentrated devices.

- [**The Quiet Year**](./comparisons/the-quiet-year) — map, deck, and temporality as generators of community complexity;
- [**Dream Askew**](./comparisons/dream-askew) — no dice and no master, distributed authority, and an economy of vulnerability;
- [**Monsterhearts 2**](./comparisons/monsterhearts-2) — deliberately narrowing mechanical space to generate highly specific relationships and complications;
- [**Ribbon Drive**](./comparisons/ribbon-drive) — playlists and music as a device that guides the story.

These games matter precisely because they prevent Resonance from claiming overly broad ideas such as “rules should serve experience” or “a light device can produce rich fiction” as novelty. The laboratory's work must remain more precise: **understand how burden is distributed and how a practicable form provides access to complexity that can exceed it.**
