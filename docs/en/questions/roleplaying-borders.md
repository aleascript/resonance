# What still makes a role-playing game?

GM. Dice. Character sheet. Spoken conversation. Individual character. Prepared scenario. Campaign.

All of these belong deeply to the history of role-playing games.

That does not mean each is a necessary condition.

One way to understand what a component does is to remove, move, or replace it.

What happens without a central GM? Without randomness? Without an individual character? When writing becomes the primary medium? When the table is replaced by asynchronous play? When the Situation itself produces part of what a scenario used to provide?

These experiments interest me less as a way to push a boundary for its own sake than as a way to discover the hidden functions behind familiar forms.

[La Voie Lunaire](../experiments/la-voie-lunaire.md) and the work on [Situation](../situation.md) have already started dismantling some GM functions.

The [historical cases](../comparisons/origins.md) ask the question in the other direction: which conventions were already present at the birth of role-playing, and which stabilized later?

The boundary itself interests me less than what we learn by crossing it.
