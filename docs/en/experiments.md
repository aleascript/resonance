# Experiments

Resonance is tested by building things with it. Projects in its lineage are therefore not only “products”: each also acts as an **experimental setup** for one or more questions in the laboratory.

This does not mean the game is secondary to theory. A Resonance experiment must first work as a game. It is precisely because it has to survive real play that it can teach the laboratory something.

## Map of the current lineage

| Project | Type | Relation to Resonance | Main questions tested |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Regard** | Playable framework | Developed through Resonance | relevance, fiction-system continuity, framework generativity |
| **Glorantha Perspectives** | Game | Built with Regard | perspective, ontology, world as a source of mechanics |
| **Scooby-Doo** | Game | Built with Regard | genre, tropes, compression, entry accessibility |
| **Unmind** | Game | Developed directly through Resonance | alternative architecture, materiality, private truths, loss and silence |

The lineage is therefore not a sequence of versions of one engine:

> **Resonance → Regard → Glorantha Perspectives / Scooby-Doo**  
> **Resonance → Unmind**

That fork matters. If every game necessarily passed through Regard, it would be difficult to know whether Resonance really names a broader paradigm or merely the meta-discourse of one framework.

## Regard — making relevance operational

[**Regard**](https://aleascript.github.io/regard) is a TTRPG framework developed through Resonance. It has a fully playable standard configuration and can also be used as a design framework.

### Experimental question

> **Can selecting what matters in the fiction become the central resolution gesture without reducing the framework to a fixed list of skills or modifiers?**

Regard separates two questions:

- **What matters here?** — relevant fictional truths become **Bets**;
- **How does this perspective understand uncertainty?** — a **Prism** can transform how resolution works.

### What Regard has already shown

Regard demonstrates that a substantial part of the mechanical interface can move from “which rule or value do I activate?” toward “what in this situation deserves to count?”

It also provides a methodological result: a framework can distinguish its **invariants**, its **standard configuration**, and choices left to the game built with it. This makes it immediately playable without presenting every default choice as universal truth.

### What it does not yet prove

Regard remains a recognizable architecture. If every implementation retains Intention, Focus, Bets, dice pools, and variations on Prisms, it may produce an excellent family of games without demonstrating Resonance's highest ambition.

Regard is therefore both a result and an **experimental risk**: it is successful enough to become a habit.

**Research fields:** experience → system; attention and perspective; entry accessibility.

## Glorantha Perspectives — making several relations to reality playable

[**Glorantha Perspectives**](https://aleascript.github.io/glorantha-perspectives) is the first game that deeply developed the branch that eventually became Regard.

### Experimental question

> **Can a world in which several cosmologies are lived as operational realities be played without reducing them to one neutral physics hidden behind the fiction?**

Glorantha was a demanding test because cultures, myths, runes, religious practices, and worldviews are not merely decoration. They shape what characters can recognize as real and possible.

The game therefore treats what a character knows, possesses, practices, and believes as distinct sources of relevance and mechanical behavior. Regard's concept of the **Prism** partly originates in this experiment.

### What Glorantha Perspectives has shown

The game made credible the idea that a system can do more than narratively describe different points of view: a perspective can become **operational in resolution itself**.

It also revealed a more radical ontological axis. Sometimes the question is not only “how does this character interpret the world?” but “does the game accept that the world effectively behaves differently through certain relations to reality?”

### Its limitation as evidence for Resonance

Glorantha Perspectives is also the laboratory's first major bias. Much of Resonance's vocabulary and intuition stabilized through this game. Treating every property of Glorantha Perspectives as a necessary Resonance invariant would therefore be circular.

The laboratory must actively seek games that **reject its solutions while preserving its questions**.

**Research fields:** experience → system; attention, relevance, perspective, and reality.

## Scooby-Doo — compressing without losing signature

[**Scooby-Doo**](https://aleascript.github.io/scooby-doo) is a lightweight game built with Regard.

### Experimental question

> **How many rules, how much lore, and how much preparation can be removed while preserving a strongly characterized experience?**

The game does not try to exhaustively simulate the Scooby-Doo setting. It tries to make **effective play naturally produce recognizable behaviors and rhythms**: comic fear, plans going wrong, asymmetric character tropes, and an investigation that progresses through chaos.

Prisms allow character tropes to function differently rather than remain colorful descriptions.

### What Scooby-Doo tests for Resonance

- whether Regard can produce an immediately legible game;
- perspectivism without strong ontological commitment;
- compression toward a very low entry cost;
- teaching genre through incentives rather than through long instructions about “good roleplay.”

### Its limitation as Game Factory evidence

Scooby-Doo intentionally remains close to Regard. It is therefore good evidence of **internal flexibility** and an important accessibility experiment, but weaker evidence that the broader Resonance method can generate radically different architectures.

**Research fields:** attention and perspective; entry accessibility; facilitation and emergence.

## Unmind — removing Regard while preserving Resonance

[**Unmind**](https://aleascript.github.io/unmind) is a short, closed, demanding game developed directly through Resonance.

It **does not use Regard**.

### Experimental question

> **If almost all of the morphology that became familiar through Regard is removed, can the result still be recognizably generated by Resonance?**

Unmind begins from its central experience: a character whose mind frays as they mobilize and reveal what constitutes them.

The mechanical answer is not a pool of Bets. The character consists of **hidden fragments arranged by depth**. Speaking from a fragment reveals it but also causes it to be lost. Taking treatment imposes silence for the situation. Silence protects temporarily, but may allow a player to **integrate** another person's words into their own structure. Lost fragments become **Traces** that the institution and place can later reuse.

### What this architecture teaches Resonance

**Relevance can work through erosion rather than accumulation.**  
In Regard, relevant truths often build a resolution. In Unmind, mobilizing a truth may precisely mean losing it.

**The device can be the mechanic.**  
Hidden stacks, depth, physical revelation, the pill-token, silence, and Traces are not illustrations of a calculation elsewhere. They constitute the activity of play.

**Perspectivism does not require ontology.**  
A statement may be true as memory, perception, or certainty for a character without automatically establishing an objective fact of the external world. A private truth can therefore be mechanically powerful while its external truth remains undecidable.

### Why Unmind is stronger evidence — but still incomplete

Unmind shows that Resonance can produce something mechanically unlike Regard while preserving a recognizable continuity: the intended experience determines the structure of relevance, interface, and possible transformations.

This is serious evidence for the generative hypothesis. But it is still one additional game, from the same laboratory and largely the same design lineage. A strong **Game Factory** claim would require more diversity, repeatability, and external appropriation.

**Research fields:** experience → system; attention and perspective; devices and materiality.

## Experiments that are not necessarily standalone games

The laboratory should not confuse “experiment” with “new product.”

Some lines of work can remain notes, procedures, or scenarios for a long time and still produce useful results.

### La Voie Lunaire and solo play

Solo practices that predate the formalization of Resonance form part of the laboratory's archaeology. They already exposed a loop in which a **Situation** produces consequences that become new Situations.

They now feed research on the boundaries of the medium, modes of play, and scenario design.

### Situation, Vector, and the GM role

This work asks whether part of the traditional scenario can be replaced by a local world under tension, then give the GM a **direction without a prescribed route**.

The experiment is not only a new preparation technique. It tests an accessibility hypothesis:

> **Can we radically reduce what a first-time GM must prepare, memorize, and arbitrate without reducing the richness of play?**

### The player's blank page

Work on lack of inspiration investigates the other side of the same interface. If a rules-light system requires players to freely invent interesting formulations at every moment, its apparent simplicity may simply move the cognitive burden.

A Resonance experiment may therefore try to **make inspiration available through the situation** rather than presuppose it.

## What a future experiment should be able to do

A new experiment becomes especially valuable when it endangers a laboratory hypothesis.

For example:

- produce a Resonance game without dice, pools, or any equivalent of a Bet;
- build two incompatible games from the same world;
- have another designer create a game from the laboratory's questions alone;
- test solo, written, or asynchronous play as the primary form rather than an adaptation;
- design specifically for first-time GMs and measure what actually needs to be transmitted;
- discover that a supposedly central principle was only a habit inherited from an earlier prototype.

> **A good R&D result is not only a game that works. It is a game that teaches the laboratory something it did not already know.**
